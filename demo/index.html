VibeProtocol

VibeProtocol demo

An open protocol that lets any app be modded by its own users, safely. A user makes a wish, their AI writes a small sandboxed program called a vibe, and the app runs it inside a box it controls.

The tour

  1. In PocketLedger, pick the wish “Flag recurring charges above $20/mo, red above 80% of budget”. Watch the scripted AI read what PocketLedger offers, write the vibe against synthetic sample data, and preview it.
  2. Review the generated permission label (Reads, Shows, Cannot) and press Allow and add.
  3. Subscription Watch runs live in the panel. Open its ⋯ menu and press Inspect to see opaque sealed handles instead of merchant or account names, plus a ledger of what it read.
  4. Pick the second wish, “Make it red above 90% and show only the worst three”, to update Subscription Watch to version 2 with no new permissions.
  5. Ask the Finance Assistant “Why did Sapphire Card jump this week?” (or try Inbox Assistant in PulseMail and Deal Assistant in CartScout). Each reply embeds a live view marked Temporary; press Pin to Vibe Space to keep it with a custom title and note.
  6. Open the ⋯ menu on a card, press Share…, and open Open as household partner in PocketLedger (or teammate/friend views in PulseMail and CartScout) to see the label again and run it against a separate dataset.
  7. Switch to Moments: run “Who are my top fans?” (ranking followers without identifying them), test a Subscription Watch link to see cross-app share refusal, and install Focus timer from Portable vibes in any of the four hosts.
  8. Manage, edit, and title your vibes: open a card’s ⋯ menu to Rename, Edit… (remix thresholds/counts/hours/discounts by wish or edit JS code with a live sandbox test), restore earlier versions, or pause, reorder, filter, and undo deletes.
  9. Graceful when data sources go offline or are revoked: disconnect Bank sync or unlink an account in PocketLedger, pause Mailbox sync in PulseMail, rate-limit Price tracker feed in CartScout, or turn off Follower likes access in Moments — cards keep their last reading calmly, let you preview sample data, and recover automatically when the source returns.

What is real here

  • Real: the sandbox, the label, sealed values (plus capability checks, the read ledger, share links, and four hosts on one kit).
  • Scripted: the AI and the data (including the assistant replies).
  • Stand-in: a browser sandbox instead of Wasm.
